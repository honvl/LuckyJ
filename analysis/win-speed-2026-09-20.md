# Win-speed review of Honver's Jade-South games against LuckyJ - 2026-09-20

## Method

Corpus: the 58 ranked Jade-South hanchan Honver (account 120182062, EN server) played
between 2026-01-25 and 2026-09-19, the period since the LuckyJ-style adjustments settled.
577 hands. Every game converts through the patched tensoul in `tools/tensoul/`, replays
cleanly, and reconciles to the server's final scores.

Baseline: LuckyJ, measured by the same code. `scripts/naga_to_tenhou.py` rebuilds all 1,255
cached NAGA reports (Tenhou, April to May 2023) as tenhou.net/6 logs, 13,054 hands, each
verified by replay and hand-to-hand score reconciliation. Where the definitions coincide, the
same-code LuckyJ numbers land on the NAGA-side miners: yakuhai pon 75.0% against 73.6%,
closed chi 7.3% against 6.7%, riichi at first tenpai on 4-7 waits 79.1% against 78.3%.

Commands:

```bash
.venv/bin/python scripts/naga_to_tenhou.py
.venv/bin/python scripts/review_win_speed.py --manifest data/local_sources/luckyj_tenhou/index.json --json analysis/win-speed-luckyj-2026-09-20.json
.venv/bin/python scripts/review_win_speed.py --since 2026-01-01 --baseline analysis/win-speed-luckyj-2026-09-20.json --json analysis/win-speed-2026-09-20.json
```

Definitions:

- turn: the seat's own discard count. A win is scored on the next draw, discards plus one.
- acceptance: unseen copies of every tile type that lowers the 13-tile hand's shanten,
  compared with the widest same-shanten discard from the same 14 tiles, on pre-tenpai
  discards with no riichi on the table. Yaku-blind and value-blind, so it is read as a
  comparison with LuckyJ rather than an error count.
- first closed tenpai: the first turn a closed hand is tenpai. "Yakuless" means no winning
  tile gives a yaku on ron without riichi (`mahjong` hand calculator).

Sample size: 58 games is enough to separate the large gaps and not the small ones. Standard
errors on Honver's cells are given with the tables. Two gaps are beyond noise: closed tenpai
kept dama (z 3.2) and win value (mangan share z 3.1, han per win z 3.4). Speed and
efficiency show no gap at all. Conversion and riichi-declaration gaps point the same way as
the dama gap and sit inside one to two standard errors.

## Headline

The imitation is faithful where it was aimed. Speed to tenpai, discard efficiency, lone
honor timing, call frequency and deal-in rate are LuckyJ's numbers. Wins also arrive on
LuckyJ's turn: 12.21 against 12.09.

What is slow is the rate at which points arrive. A Honver win is worth 5,762 points against
LuckyJ's 7,269, 2.89 han against 3.37, and 12.7% of wins reach mangan against 21.9%. Closed
tenpai is kept dama 35.3% of the time against 23.0%, and once declined, riichi almost never
follows. Tenpai converts to a win 41.9% against 47.0%, with part of that gap coming from the
room: opponents at Jade finish against a live tenpai more often than LuckyJ's 2023 Tenhou
opponents did.

LuckyJ loses acceptance early for the same reason Honver does: 207.8 per 100 discards on
turns 1-3, 20.3% of narrower discards keeping a value honor. The honor-holding habit is
LuckyJ's habit, copied.

The blind count treats every kept dora, honor or tanyao shape as a loss. A value-aware
version measures each discard only against alternatives that keep at least as many dora and
red fives, the same value honors, the same guest winds, and no more terminals or honors.
What remains is acceptance given up with nothing identifiable in return:

Honver gives up less for nothing than LuckyJ does in every turn window. Efficiency is
settled and is no lever. LuckyJ's larger unexplained loss late in the hand is most likely
safety on number tiles, which the feature set does not credit.

Three things describe LuckyJ's method:

1. It comes from the 3-4 yaochuu deals, a third of all hands. With one or two honors and one
   or two terminals, LuckyJ has the last honor out by turn 5 and is fully clean by turn 7.5;
   Honver holds the last honor to turn 6.3 and is clean by 8.4. The order is the same for
   both: in deals whose only terminals and honors are lone honors and isolated 1s and 9s,
   guest winds and isolated terminals go on turn 1 (Honver: turn 1 and turn 2), and the lone
   value honor goes last, LuckyJ at median turn 3 (turn 4 when the deal is 4-shanten or
   worse), Honver at turn 4 (turn 5 in slow deals). LuckyJ cuts that honor with a riichi
   already out in 5% of these hands, Honver in 11%, 15% in slow deals: held long enough to
   become the defensive tile instead of a discard. The result is 8.2% tanyao wins from those
   deals against 2.4%, and clean by turn 6 in 18.5% against 12.7%. Overall win rate from
   those deals is the same (26.0% against 24.9%); the difference is which yaku the hand ends
   with, and the win is worth 1,400 more.
2. A yakuhai pair is ponned, by both players. Neither goes tanyao from a hand that starts
   with a pair of dragons or seat wind. The tanyao decision is about lone honors.
3. The finish is ordinary. Open share, chi as the first call, dora count at the deal and in
   the win are all the same. LuckyJ does not build tanyao with more dora; it gets there a
   turn earlier and converts the clean hand at twice the rate, 22.3% against 11.4%.

## How to fix it

1. Riichi at the first closed tenpai at LuckyJ's rates: 82% on 4-7 waits, 50% on 3 or fewer,
   when nobody has declared. At Honver's current closed-tenpai count that is about 19 more
   riichi per 58 games. Using LuckyJ's own outcomes for each choice, each of those hands is
   worth roughly 1,800 points more in expectation, about 600 points a game. When the first
   tenpai is declined, keep the option open: LuckyJ declares later in 17% of declined hands,
   Honver in 5%.
2. Build tanyao from the 3-4 yaochuu deals. When the deal holds one or two lone honors and
   one or two isolated terminals, no yakuhai pair, and simples elsewhere: isolated terminals
   and guest winds on turn 1, and the lone value honor by turn 3, turn 4 at the latest in a
   slow hand. LuckyJ does hold that honor last, for two turns, and releases it while the
   table is still quiet. Holding it to turn 5 is what turns it into a defensive tile and
   leaves the hand a turn behind. Tanyao at 21.8% of wins instead of 11.1% is
   where LuckyJ's mangan share and open-hand value come from. Discard efficiency is settled
   and is no part of this; the value-aware count shows Honver already gives up less for
   nothing than LuckyJ.
3. Keep dora in the hand's plan. A third of wins carry no dora at all against 28%, and dora
   han per win is 1.14 against 1.32. This is a smaller lever than the first two.

Leave alone: tenpai speed, discard efficiency, lone honor timing, call frequency and defense.
They match LuckyJ already, and the earlier opponent-based note about cutting value honors
earlier is withdrawn; LuckyJ holds them exactly as long.

## Deal-ins into called hands

Exposure is the same at both tables: 33% of discards are made while an opponent has at least
one meld and no riichi is out, for Honver and for LuckyJ alike. Deal-ins per 100 discards
made in each situation:

| facing | Honver | LuckyJ |
|---|---:|---:|
| a riichi | 1.59 | 1.76 |
| an open hand with 2 or more melds | 1.82 | 1.47 |
| an open hand with 1 meld | 1.54 | 1.01 |
| 2 or more melds, turn 8 or later | 2.18 | 1.74 |

Of Honver's 38 deal-ins into open hands, 23 were into one-meld hands. The winners' yaku were
yakuhai in 27 cases (haku 8, seat wind 7, chun 5, hatsu 4, round wind 3) and tanyao in 11.
Honver was tenpai or 1-shanten in 32 of the 38, the tile was a 2 to 8 in 33, and 58% were
chosen discards rather than tsumogiri (LuckyJ: 43%). The leak is the single yakuhai pon
from turn 9 on, played as if it were still a closed table.

## When LuckyJ declares after passing

LuckyJ passed on the first closed tenpai 896 times and declared later in 149 (17%; Honver
3 of 58). The late riichi hands: first tenpai at median turn 7 with 3.2 live winning tiles,
yakuless 40% of the time, a riichi already out 11%. Riichi came at turn 9, one turn later
in 46% of cases and within four turns in 88%. The hand stayed tenpai in 93% of them and the
wait changed in 86%: live tiles rose to 4.7 on average, yakuless fell to 26%. Those riichi
then won 49% and dealt in 13%, a normal riichi. The 747 hands that never declared had a
later first tenpai (turn 10), a riichi out 31% of the time, and won 38% dama.

So LuckyJ's pass is a two-turn clock on an early bad wait: keep tenpai, reshape, declare the
turn the wait becomes two-sided. Passes that stay dama are late tenpai or tenpai under a
riichi.

Everything here is descriptive. Honver's cells carry their standard errors; LuckyJ's are
from 13,054 hands and are tight.
