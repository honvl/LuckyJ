The sixteen points

  1. 01Put Placement Before UkeireBefore counting ukeire, decide what this hanchan needs from the hand: a cheap finish, a bigger hand, or a fold.
  2. 02Do Not Choose the Hand Too EarlyEarly, prefer the tile with two jobs, shape plus yaku, value, a call, or safety, over the tile that only improves one narrow version of the hand.
  3. 03Call Only When the Call Changes the HandCall when the call gives you a yaku, tenpai, a real one-shanten, a denied draw, or a safe path to keiten, and that is worth more than the closed premium you give up.
  4. 04Keep Brakes on Open HandsAfter every call, know which tile lets the open hand stop or keep going safely.
  5. 05Cut the Tile That Will Get WorseIn a slow hand, discard the floater whose danger will rise fastest, unless it is needed for value, yaku, or your best one-shanten shape.
  6. 06Buy Value While It Is CheapKeep dora, red-five, and yakuhai value early while it still improves a real winning line.
  7. 07Make Riichi Tax the TableDeclare riichi when the added value and the forced reactions change what the hand is worth.
  8. 08Reprice the Whole Push Every DrawAfter every draw, call, riichi, and new safe tile, count the dangerous discards still ahead.
  9. 09The Third Row Is a Counting DrillIn the third row, choose among three outcomes only: winning tenpai, safe tenpai for the draw, or a real fold.
  10. 10Treat Keiten as AttackIn late hands, chase keiten while safe tiles keep you tenpai.
  11. 11Make Engine Splits Prove Their CaseWhen LuckyJ and an engine split, sort the hand as blunder, style trade-off, or genuine insight.
  12. 12Price the Missing YakuhaiAfter an unclear call, mark live dragons, the round wind, and that player's seat wind as possible yaku tiles.
  13. 13Name Who Your Safe Tile DefendsKeep a genbutsu or credible suji only when you can say which opponent it answers and which bad turn it covers.
  14. 14Spend Safety When Its Opponent DisappearsWhenever the main danger changes, ask who the safe tile still protects you from.
  15. 15The Edge Tile Can Be the AttackWhen choosing between an outside tile and a middle connector, keep the connector if it is the tile that makes your real tenpai or win possible.
  16. 16Label Every Honor Before You Move ItBefore cutting or keeping an honor, label it: self value, opponent's yaku, dead tile, or defense.

How the book reads a hand

NAGA is the review lens. Its model heads, Nishiki, Hibakari, and Kagashi, show strong candidate lines and mark the moments where LuckyJ chose another plan. Those disagreements are review prompts, not automatic improvements on ordinary efficient play. LuckyJ still matches Nishiki’s top discard about 73% of the time.
