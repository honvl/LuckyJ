Where your points go against LuckyJ.

This guide measures 80 of your Jade South games since January 2026, 830 hands, with the same code that measured 1,255 LuckyJ games. It keeps the lessons from our reviews and shows your own table at each turn where a different tile, call or declaration would have played closer to LuckyJ.

Most of your game already matches. Tenpai speed, tile efficiency, call rate and honor timing are LuckyJ's numbers, and you fold against a riichi at least as well as LuckyJ does. The gap is in which hands you finish and what they are worth, plus a small set of tile choices once someone has called.

Your first rate since January, 23.8%, sits within one standard error of your career 26.3% over 3,877 games. The Jade players at your tables win, deal in and score like LuckyJ's Tokujou opponents. The difference that is real is LuckyJ's 31.7% firsts against your career 26.3%, and it lives in the value of your wins.

Safety labels read from your seat at that turn. genbutsu is in that player's river, or was discarded by anyone after their riichi without being ronned; those tiles also anchor suji. suji is a 1, 2 or 3 (or 7, 8 or 9) whose partner three away is anchored. nakasuji is a 4, 5 or 6 with both partners anchored; with only one it is half suji, and still mostly live. A 4 whose 1 is anchored, or a 6 whose 9 is, reads as virtual nakasuji when that player also cut the 5 early: the early 5 is the sotogawa read that covers the other side. An honor with two other copies showing can only be hit by a tanki. Everything else is live.

Count the dora before you pick the plan

LuckyJ wins about as many hands as you do. The difference is what it does with a hand that has dora. Sorted by the dora and red fives in the deal, your win rate stays flat while LuckyJ's climbs:

On hands with no dora you already play like LuckyJ, and you win them slightly more often. On deals with one dora or more, LuckyJ makes 8.8 mangan per 100 hands and you make 3.9; on deals with none, 4.1 and 3.8. Dora reach your hand at the same rate and you both cut about a fifth of them, so the gap comes from three habits.

Riichi at the first closed tenpai. With nobody in riichi, LuckyJ declares 51% of first tenpais on 3 or fewer live tiles, 82% on 4 to 7 and 93% on 8 or more. You declare 41%, 76% and 89%. Your 20 dama wins average 5,880 and 20% reach mangan; LuckyJ's average 7,667 and 36% reach mangan, because it keeps dama mostly for hands that are already big.

Shape yaku on top of the dora. LuckyJ wins 5.0 tanyao hands per 100 and you win 3.0; honitsu 2.1 against 1.3. In deals with lone honors and isolated terminals, LuckyJ has cleared every terminal and honor by turn 6 in 35% of hands and wins 28% of those deals. You clear 22% and win 17%. Junk goes first: guest winds and isolated terminals on turn 1, the lone value honor by turn 3 or 4.

A yakuhai pon needs something behind it. When your first call is a yakuhai pon, 47% of the time the hand has no dora (LuckyJ 41%). Your pon hands with two or more dora win 28% of the time against LuckyJ's 43%, though that is only 18 of your hands. A bare yakuhai pon from 3-shanten earns LuckyJ about 16 points per hand, which is nothing. Let the first copy go when the pon would leave you 3-shanten with no dora, unless you lead or the hand has a second plan such as toitoi or honitsu.

Rule Count the dora and red fives on turn 1. None: a cheap hand to finish if it comes and fold first when someone attacks. One: a mangan candidate, so riichi at the first tenpai and build tanyao when the deal allows. Two or more: the hand of the game, so call when the call lands you at 1-shanten or tenpai, riichi at the first closed tenpai and push it at tenpai.
